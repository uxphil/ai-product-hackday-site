Working with your AI

Talk to it like a colleague

Think of your AI as a very fast colleague who’s never met you. It knows a lot about building things and nothing about who you’re designing for. That’s your job.

You don’t need special words. Brief it the way you’d brief a colleague. If the answer isn’t right, say so. It won’t be offended.

Ask it for help with anything, including “how do I…?”

  • Tell it who you are. “I’m a designer. Explain things in plain words.”
  • Give it context. Who it’s for, the problem, and what good looks like.
  • Keep talking. Ask follow-up questions, and say “that’s not what I meant”.
  • Let it question you. Ask it to ask you questions before it builds anything.

Working with your AI

Show it, don’t describe it

A screenshot beats a paragraph. If something looks wrong, or you see an error you don’t understand, grab it, paste it and ask “what’s going on here?”

On a Mac

Grab part of the screen. Press ⌘ Cmd ⇧ Shift 4, then drag a box round the bit you need.

Put it in the chat. Drag the thumbnail (bottom right) into the chat. Or add Ctrl to copy it, then press ⌘ Cmd V.

On Windows

Grab part of the screen. Press ⊞ Win ⇧ Shift S, then drag a box round the bit you need.

Put it in the chat. It’s copied already. Click in the chat and press Ctrl V.

Grab only the bit you need. A full-screen shot can catch Teams, email, notifications and other tabs. Share only a screenshot of your own sample product.

Working with your AI

Stay in charge

AI can be confidently wrong. The cure is simple: small steps, and look at the result every time.

One change at a time

Ask, check, then the next thing.

Check it yourself

Open it, click it, try it on your phone. “Done” doesn’t always mean done.

Save when it works

Publish a working version before a big change.

If in doubt, ask it to explain its choices. “What were the options? Why this one?” That’s how you learn, and it’s often how you catch the mistake.

Going round in circles? Start a fresh chat and explain where you’ve got to.

Working with your AI

Permissions, money and getting stuck

These tools can create files, run commands and use your accounts. That’s what makes them useful, and it’s why you read before you say yes.

Read before you say yes

Your AI asks permission before it does things. Say yes to your project folder and GitHub. Say no to anything you don’t understand, then ask for help.

Don’t buy anything on the spot

Asked to pay, upgrade or add a card without planning to? Stop and ask. Ran out of usage? Don’t top up in a panic.

The 10-minute rule

Stuck for ten minutes? Ask for help. Screenshot, paste, ask: your AI is the first helper you go to.

Never paste a password, key or token into a chat. If your AI asks for one, say no. GitHub gives you a one-time code to type in yourself.

The take-away7 / 8
Stop 1 of 5 · Warm-up

Make your first page

  1. Install the app and sign in. In Claude, click the Code tab. In the ChatGPT app, choose Codex from the dropdown.
  2. Create a free GitHub account at github.com/signup, with your own email. Your username is public. Remember how you signed up (email, Google or Apple) and always sign in the same way.
  3. Give it an empty folder. Make a new folder called hello-hackday on your Desktop and open it in your tool.
  4. Ask for your page. Paste this in. It may ask a question or two, or just make the page: either is fine. If it asks, answer in your own words, or say “you decide”.

    I’d like you to create a webpage that simply says hello world.

  5. See it. Ask “Please open the page in my browser”, or double-click the file ending in .html. The address starts with file://, which means it’s only on your computer.
