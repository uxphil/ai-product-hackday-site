Get ready · LBG laptop route

Take part on your LBG laptop

No personal laptop? If your LBG laptop has a Figma Make licence, you can take part on it. You plan your product with Microsoft Copilot and build and publish it in Figma Make. The warm-up takes about 15 minutes (an estimate).

Please read this firstYou’re on an LBG laptop, so LBG’s normal rules for this machine and its tools apply. Use fictional or public sample content only, even though you’re at work: no customer, colleague or internal information in prompts, files or anything you publish. More on staying safe.

What’s different on this route

  • It shares inside LBG only. Anything you publish from LBG’s Figma opens only for colleagues signed in to LBG’s Figma. It won’t open on your personal phone or for anyone outside LBG.
  • Your work stays with LBG. Your project lives in LBG’s Figma account, so you can’t take it home the way you can with a personal GitHub account.
  • No GitHub, no installs. Everything happens in Figma and Copilot, which you already have.
  • AI credits. Figma Make uses AI credits from your licence. How many you have for a full day is To be confirmed. If they run out, tell Phil or a helper.

These steps follow Figma’s documentation and haven’t yet been tried by us on an LBG laptop. Button names may differ slightly.

  1. Read the safety note1 min

    It’s the box at the top of this page.

  2. Open Figma Make2 min

    Open Figma on your LBG laptop, signed in with your work account as usual. In the file browser, create a new Make file.

    Can’t see Make, or it asks you to request access? Your licence may not include it. Message Phil on Teams before the day, or come to a setup clinic.

  3. Ask for the page5 min

    Paste this into the Make chat.

    Create a single-page website with a heading that says "Hello, world", one friendly sentence, and a button that changes the sentence when clicked. Use sample text only. No sign-in, database or other services.

    Click the button in the preview to check it works.

  4. Publish it inside LBG3 min

    Click Publish. If Figma asks who can see it, choose your organisation (LBG only). Publish, then copy the address it gives you.

  5. Check someone else can open it2–5 min

    Send the address to a colleague on Teams and ask them to open it. They may need to be signed in to Figma. That proves another person can open your work, which is what the day’s finish line asks for.

You’re ready

  • Your licence includes Figma Make.
  • You built a page and published it inside LBG.
  • A colleague could open it.

Bring your LBG laptop and charger on the day. You’ll start a new Make file for your real project.

Saved on this device only

On the day: plan in Copilot, build in Make

You follow the same Plan your product exercise as everyone else. Only the tools change.

  1. Question your idea with Copilotabout 15 min

    Open Microsoft Copilot on your LBG laptop and paste in the prompt from Plan your product. It works with any assistant. Answer in your own words, and push back when you disagree.

  2. Save your PRD as a Markdown fileabout 5 min

    Once you’ve agreed the PRD, ask Copilot:

    Please write the agreed PRD in Markdown, inside a single code block, so I can copy it into a file.

    Copy it, open Notepad and paste. Choose File → Save as, set Save as type to All files, name it PRD.md and save it in your OneDrive.

    Prefer Word? That’s fine: save it as a Word document instead. Markdown is worth a try, though (see below).

  3. Hand the PRD to Figma Make2 min

    Start a new Make file for your project. Paste in your PRD with this message on top:

    Here is the PRD for what we’re building. Read it, then tell me the first small slice you’d build and wait for my go-ahead before you start.

  4. Build in small slicesthe rest of the day

    Get one piece working, check it against the PRD, then move on. When you learn something, update PRD.md in Notepad and tell Make what changed.

Why Markdown, not Word?

Markdown is plain text with a few symbols for structure: # for a heading, - for a list item. It’s worth getting used to because:

  • AI tools read it exactly. There’s no hidden formatting, so headings and lists arrive as you wrote them.
  • One file works everywhere. Copilot, Figma Make, Claude, ChatGPT and GitHub all read it.
  • It’s readable as it is. Open it in Notepad and it still makes sense.
  • It’s where AI building is heading. Many AI build tools keep their plans and instructions in .md files, so it’s a useful habit for later.

The finish line on this route

Everyone works towards the same six things. Here’s how they look for you.

  • A live address. Published from Make inside LBG, and opened by a colleague on their own laptop.

  • Your reviewed PRD. Saved as PRD.md (or a Word document) in your OneDrive, rather than on GitHub.

  • Working in a pair? Share the Make file with your partner inside LBG so you can both open and edit it.

  • Change and publish again. Make a change in Make, publish again and check your address shows it.
