Theme: Elf Help

An Elf on the Shelf planner

Plan the elf. Keep the magic.

  • ForA busy parent or carer who does the Elf on the Shelf
  • You’ll buildA seven-day plan, one trick per day
  • AI featuresOptional. Sample ideas are enough.
  • Good if you likePlayful content, tone and interaction

Please read this firstThis is a personal learning exercise. Use fictional or public sample content only. Do not enter, upload or share any LBG data in AI tools, GitHub repositories, prompts, screenshots or published websites. More on staying safe.

Your actual project starts on the day. This page is a short brief to help you choose a theme. Please don’t start designing or building beforehand. On the day you’ll plan it with AI first.

The problem we’re solving

It’s 11pm, the elf hasn’t moved, and I’m out of ideas.

Many a parent, some time in December

The Elf on the Shelf visits for weeks, and the ideas run out long before December does. Parents and carers end up searching at 11pm for something quick, or discovering the supplies they need are missing. They want variety and a bit of wow, without the nightly scramble.

Who it’s for

The planner

A busy parent or carer who does the Elf on the Shelf and wants a manageable trick for each day.

Grown-ups only

Keep it about the adult doing the planning. You don’t need any child profiles or real family details to build or test this.

A taste of the tricks

Three sample ideas, to show the kind of content a planner needs. You’ll write your own set on the day.

Paper snowball fight

The elf takes on the teddies with scrunched-up paper snowballs.

Supplies: scrap paper, two soft toys

5 min prep

Elf zip-line

The elf whizzes across the room on a string, riding in a paper cup.

Supplies: string, a paper cup, sticky tape

10 min prep

Note from the North Pole

A tiny letter from the elf, with news from the workshop.

Supplies: paper, a pen

2 min prep

First version, in scope

  1. See the week

    One suggested trick per day for seven days, from a small curated set of sample ideas.

  2. Know what’s needed

    Each trick shows a short description, preparation time and supplies.

  3. Swap one

    Replace any single trick.

  4. Print it

    Print the resulting plan.

Product considerations

Suggestions to think about, not requirements. Use the ones that help.

  • Effort and spectacle. Some nights need a two-minute trick, others a showpiece. Let the planner say how much effort and how much “wow” they want, and explain the choices with short examples.

  • Know what’s needed up front. Show prep time and supplies for each trick, so they can be bought or found ahead of time.

  • Swap without side effects. Replacing one day’s trick must not change the other days.

  • Printable. A plan for the fridge or a notebook is a core need. Remember it’s for the grown-ups, so keep it away from small eyes.

  • Safe by default. Keep sample tricks away from anything hot, sharp, a choking risk or an allergy risk.

  • Warm and playful tone. It should feel like a helpful friend, not a project tool.

  • Saving. If you save plans in the browser, say plainly that it works on that browser only and is not synced across devices.

  • AI is optional. A small set of well-written sample ideas delivers the whole first version.

How you’ll know it works

  • You can see a suggested trick for each date.
  • You can replace one without changing the other dates.
  • You can review its supplies.
  • You can print the updated plan.
  • If you save in the browser: refresh and check it’s still there.

If you finish early, stretch ideas

  • Edit or re-roll. Edit a trick, or ask for a different suggestion for a chosen day.

  • Tailor it. Tailor suggestions by prep time, complexity, how elaborate the setup is and desired wow factor.

  • Shopping list. Filter by supplies you already have, budget or mess tolerance, and combine supplies into a shopping list.

  • Supply links. Link to shops or searches for supplies. No checkout and no live prices unless you have a real source.

  • All 24 days. Stretch to all 24 days.

  • Advanced routes. AI-generated suggestions, live product search or saving across devices. These are advanced routes: see the advanced section first.

What to avoid

  • Children’s names, photos or any real family details.
  • Checkout, payment or accounts.
  • Implying that prices or stock are live when they aren’t.
  • Needing AI, a database or an API key for the first version.
  • Building all 24 days before seven days work.
