Project themes

Pick a theme that appeals, or bring your own idea. Your actual project starts on the day. Please don’t begin designing or building it beforehand. On the day you’ll plan it with AI before you build.

Please read this firstThis is a personal learning exercise. Use fictional or public sample content only. Do not enter, upload or share any LBG data in AI tools, GitHub repositories, prompts, screenshots or published websites. More on staying safe.

Three prepared themes, plus your own

Personal website

For: a visitor who wants to know who you are and how to contact you.

First version: one responsive page with an introduction, two or three sections and a working contact link.

Safe default: a fictional persona or public sample content.

Elf Help

An Elf on the Shelf planner

Plan the elf. Keep the magic.

For: a busy parent or carer who does the Elf on the Shelf and wants a manageable trick for each day, without the late-night panic.

First version: one suggested trick per day for seven days, from a small set of sample ideas. Each shows a short description, prep time and supplies. Swap any trick you don’t like, then print the plan. AI is optional: sample ideas are enough.

Ideas to go further
  • Edit a trick, or ask for a different one for a chosen day.
  • Choose how much effort, setup and “wow factor” you want.
  • Filter by supplies you already have, budget or mess; build a shopping list.
  • Link to shops or searches for supplies (no checkout, no live prices).
  • Stretch to all 24 days, or try the advanced routes below.

No real family or child details.

Bring your own idea

Choose one user, one job and no more than three essential capabilities.

Aim for something you can publish for free as a website. Helpers may suggest trimming anything that needs payments or private data, or that puts your first release at risk.

What every core theme has in common

  • It can be a simple, static website. AI features are optional.

  • It uses fictional or public sample content only.

  • It’s published at an address someone else can open.

  • It’s small enough to finish in one day, with ideas for what next.

Advanced routes, if you want a challenge

Two optional ideas for people happy with extra setup and troubleshooting. You don’t need either one, and no purchase is needed to complete a core theme.

Shared idea board

For: a small, made-up hobby group keeping track of activity ideas.

First version: add an idea with a title and description, list ideas, and move each between “suggested”, “planned” and “done”. Ideas are saved in a hosted database, so they survive a reload and show up in a second browser.

Check it: add an idea, reload, change its status, then see the change in another browser.

Database provider To be confirmed. Set access rules so strangers can’t delete or flood your board. Sample content only.

Needs an API key

Film-night finder

For: someone planning a film night who wants to search films and make a shortlist.

First version: search a real film catalogue, see a few details for each match, and save a shortlist in the browser. Clear messages when nothing matches or the service is down.

Proposed catalogue: The Movie Database (TMDB). Its API is free for non-commercial use if you credit TMDB in your app. You create your own TMDB account and request a key in its settings. TMDB FAQ and attribution rules.

Needs a small server-side function to hide the key, so check your host supports one.

Before you pick one

  • One new thing at a time. Choose a database or an API, not both.

  • Keep keys secret. Store an API key in your host’s secret settings. Never paste it into a prompt, a repository file or code that runs in the browser.

  • Check costs and limits first. A free API doesn’t mean free hosting or instant access. Any spending is your choice.

  • Have a fallback. If setup stalls, sample data keeps your demo working. Label it clearly as sample data, not a live result.
