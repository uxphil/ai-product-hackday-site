Plan your product

Before you build, spend about 25 minutes thinking your idea through with AI. The goal isn’t a finished product from one clever prompt. It’s a small, clear plan that you understand and have agreed.

Please read this firstThis is a personal learning exercise. Use fictional or public sample content only. Do not enter, upload or share any LBG data in AI tools, GitHub repositories, prompts, screenshots or published websites. More on staying safe.

This happens on the day. Please don’t start your project beforehand. The hello-world warm-up only checks your tools work; it isn’t how you plan a real product.

First, start your real project

Your warm-up was a rehearsal. Leave it as it is and start fresh. Not sure how to do any of this? Ask your AI tool to guide you one step at a time, or ask a helper.

  1. Make a new folder for your product (or a new project, if you use a builder such as Lovable). Don’t reuse hello-hackday.
  2. Create a new GitHub repository for it. Builders connect to GitHub in different ways, so follow your tool’s own steps. In Claude or Codex, you can paste the prompt below.
  3. Keep the warm-up separate. Its repository isn’t your product, so don’t build on it.
  4. Save your reviewed PRD.md in the new repository once you’ve agreed it (step 3 of your 25 minutes, below).
  5. Working in a pair? Make sure you both contribute and both can open and edit the project. On GitHub, the owner can invite their partner from the repository’s Settings → Collaborators.

I’m starting a new product project in this empty folder. It will only hold fictional or public sample content. Please guide me one step at a time, in plain language, to create a new public GitHub repository for it in my own account and connect this folder to it. Ask before installing anything, and never ask me for a password or token.

How the work fits together

AI helps you question, plan and build. You stay responsible for the decisions.

  1. Question

    Who is it for, and why?

  2. Agree

    Review and save a short PRD

  3. Build

    In small slices, checking each one

  4. Test

    Someone else tries it

  5. Improve

    Change the plan when you learn

Your 25 minutes

  1. Question the ideaabout 10 min

    Open your AI tool, replace [idea] with a sentence about your idea, and paste this in. It works with any assistant; you don’t need to install anything.

    Help me think through this product before we build it. My idea is [idea]. Start with the intended user, their situation and the problem. Ask a few focused questions at a time; wait for my answers before asking questions that depend on them. Challenge vague claims and suggest alternatives, but let me make the decisions. Cover the main user journey, what makes the idea useful, assumptions we cannot yet prove, accessibility, the smallest useful release, what to leave out, and how we will test success. Keep the conversation within about ten minutes; record unresolved questions as assumptions instead of inventing answers. Then propose a concise PRD and ask me to review and agree it before building. Do not generate the implementation yet.

    Answer in your own words. If it challenges you, that’s the point: push back when you disagree, and say “I don’t know yet” rather than inventing an answer. Unknowns become assumptions to test.

  2. Choose the journey and scopeabout 5 min

    Pick one user, one complete task they can do from start to finish, and no more than three essential features. Decide what you’re leaving out, and say why.

  3. Draft, challenge and save your PRDabout 10 min

    Let the AI draft it, then read it properly and fix anything that isn’t what you meant. Aim for one or two pages covering:

    • the user and their problem, and the outcome you want
    • assumptions you still need to test
    • the main user journey
    • what’s in the first release, and why
    • what’s left out
    • three to five checks that show it works
    • how you’ll publish it, and what comes next

    Save it as PRD.md in your new project’s GitHub repository, not the warm-up one.

Ready to build? Check you can explain

  • Who needs it, and why. One real-sounding person and the problem they have.

  • One complete task. Walk through it step by step, start to finish.

  • Why this first release. What’s in, what’s out, and the reason.

  • How you’ll check it. The three to five checks from your PRD.

Helpers will ask about these. A list of features with no clear user need is a sign to go back a step.

While you build

  • Build in small slices. Get one piece working, check it against your PRD, then move on.

  • Update the plan. When building or testing teaches you something, change the PRD. That’s good design, not failure.

  • Know the finish line. Six things to have by the end of the day, starting with a live address someone else can open.

  • Polish isn’t proof. A good-looking result doesn’t show people need it. Peer testing tells you what to improve next.

Where this prompt comes from

It’s adapted from Matt Pocock’s grill-me skill, made shorter and gentler for a timed exercise. Installing that skill is optional and never required.
